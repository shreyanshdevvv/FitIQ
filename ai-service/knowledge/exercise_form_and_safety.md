# Exercise Form, Spinal Mechanics, and Injury Prevention

## The Valsalva Maneuver and Core Bracing
Under heavy multi-joint spinal loading (squats, deadlifts, overhead presses), maintaining intra-abdominal pressure (IAP) protects the lumbar vertebrae:
1. Inhale deeply into the belly (diaphragmatic expansion 360 degrees).
2. Tighten abdominal walls, obliques, and erector spinae as if preparing for a punch.
3. Hold this pressurized brace through the eccentric and through the sticking point of the concentric movement.
4. Exhale controlled air at the lockout of the repetition.

## Joint Alignment and Movement Cues
- **Squats**: Knees should track directly in line with the second and third toes. Avoid knee valgus (caving inward). Maintain equal foot pressure across three points: big toe base, pinky toe base, and heel (the "tripod foot").
- **Hinging / Deadlifts**: Initiate motion by pushing hips backward toward the wall behind you rather than dropping the knees. Maintain a rigid, neutral lumbar spine to prevent shear force on spinal discs.
- **Horizontal Pressing (Bench Press)**: Retract and depress scapulae into the bench pad to stabilize the glenohumeral joint. Keep wrists stacked straight directly above the elbows, elbows tucked at approximately 45 to 70 degrees from the torso.
